Chapter 0 Executive summary

A minute-by-minute record of every campus printer

EveryoneIT leadership

Wepa publishes a status page for BSU's print stations that refreshes every minute and only ever shows now. BSU Student Printing Ops reads it once a minute, keeps every reading, and turns the history into answers: what is broken, for how long, why, when nobody was there to fix it, what to restock, and where a student should walk instead.

What it watches31 stations: 16 ResNet printers in and around the residence halls, 14 in Student Computer Labs, 1 at a satellite campus.
How oftenOne reading every 60 seconds, every reading kept. Failed reads are kept too, so gaps are visible rather than guessed.
Who it servesStudents, ResNet and IT Service Center staff, supervisors and IT leadership, each with their own view.
How it is builtPython, pandas and Dash; about 20,500 lines of application code and 190 automated tests; deployed on Azure Container Apps.

What it does

  • Now: every station's status in one vocabulary (Operational, Degraded, Out of service, No signal), a ranked work queue, and a walking or van route to everything that needs a visit.
  • Then: availability, time to repair, time between failures, faults by cause and hour, supplies and parts, all with stated definitions and minimum-evidence rules.
  • Why: plain-language stories, click-to-explain charts, statistical tests, and an optional AI analyst that may only state figures it looked up.
  • Next: supply reorder points, end-of-life forecasts for parts, and an outage-risk model that is only shown when it beats a simple baseline.
  • Accountability: permanent reference numbers for every outage, governed investigations with a tamper-evident trail for Wepa cases, named staff accounts and an audit log.
  • Self-maintenance: daily vulnerability checks ranked by real-world exploitation, reviewed update packages, backup collectors and a self-check.
Chapter 1 CRISP-DM phase 1 · Business understanding

Business understanding

SupervisorsIT leadershipEveryone
In short

Students need to print, often late and close to a deadline. When a printer breaks, the student finds out at the printer and staff find out later. The product exists to shorten that gap, to show where the gaps come from, and to give the university evidence to act on, without adding work for student staff.

1.1 Background

Bridgewater State University offers Wepa print stations across campus: in residence-hall areas (supported by ResNet, the residential network team based in East Campus Commons), and in labs, the library, the student union and a satellite campus (supported by the IT Service Center in Maxwell Library). Students pay per page through Wepa and print papers, forms, tickets and boarding passes.

Wepa publishes a public status page listing every station with a color (green, yellow, red), alert codes, printer text and consumable levels. It refreshes every minute and shows only the present. The original version of this product (2020) was a terminal script for ResNet Support Representatives: choose your assigned area and it beeped when a printer there went down.

1.2 Problem statement

Nobody could answer basic operational questions with evidence: which printers break most; how long outages last and why; whether problems wait overnight because the desks are closed; what supplies will run out next; and where a student should go when their printer is down. Without history, recurring hardware faults looked like bad luck, and conversations with the vendor relied on memory.

2.3 Known quirks

  • Severity comes from the row color. Wepa's own green, yellow or red verdict is used directly. Codes add a name and a fix category; only a row with no color falls back to the code table.
  • Codes carry details. Alert_Paper_Low_Letter_530_sheets_left is folded into paper_low so counts don't split by sheet count.
  • Only some codes have been seen live. Three red codes and one yellow code are confirmed on BSU's page; the rest come from the page's legend and are marked "inferred" until they appear.
  • Levels jitter. Sensors wobble a point or two (51, 50, 51, 50); a replacement jumps by 15 or more points.
  • The page can change. Columns are matched by header text. A rename or reorder fails the read loudly and is logged, instead of writing values into the wrong fields.
  • Reads fail sometimes. Network blips, Wepa maintenance and restarts all happen. Every attempt is logged, so a gap is a measured fact.

2.5 Data quality

Data quality is measured, scored and shown on the System page, so every figure can be read with its reliability in mind.

For data scientistsObserved time is explicit. Each reading covers the time until the station's next reading, up to 5 minutes; anything longer is unobserved and excluded from every rate. Availability is therefore a ratio of observed minutes, never of wall-clock minutes.
For supervisorsA low data-quality day means that day's numbers rest on less evidence, not that they are wrong. The System page shows which days, and the live log shows why.

3.2 Cleaning and construction rules

One reading per minute
When two collectors overlap (a deploy, a backup), duplicates for the same station and minute are dropped; for the scrape log, a success wins over a failure.
Observed time
A reading covers the time to the next one, up to 5 minutes. Longer gaps are unobserved: never counted as up or down.
Incidents
Consecutive readings of one station in the same state. An incident opens at the first reading showing it and resolves at the first that doesn't. Gaps under 6 hours with the same state on both sides are bridged.
Censored starts
An incident already open when monitoring began has an unknown start. It is kept (it is real) but excluded from repair-time averages.
Replacements
A rise of 15 points or more is a part replacement, logged with what the old part had left, and never counted as negative usage.
Usage
Between replacements, usage is the first reading minus the lowest reading so far, so jitter is counted once. 100 points equals one part's worth.
Desk hours
Each outage is split into time while its team's desk was staffed and time after hours, using the configured hours and the academic calendar's holidays.
Reference numbers
Every outage gets a permanent number by its cause: OUT unreachable, JAM jammed, PAP paper, SUP consumable out, ERR hardware or system; investigations INV, update packages UPD, feature requests REQ. Nine digits; never reused.

Chapter 4 CRISP-DM phase 4 · Modeling

Modeling

Data scientistsSupervisorsIT leadership
In short

Most of the product's value comes from careful definitions, not exotic models: what counts as available, how long an outage really lasted, what usage really was. On top of those sit a handful of well-understood statistical methods, each chosen to answer one operational question, and one machine-learning model that has to prove itself before it is shown.

4.1 Measures

MeasureDefinitionShown when
AvailabilityObserved printer-minutes not out of service ÷ all observed printer-minutesAt least half the window observed
Building coverageShare of minutes at least one printer in the building could printSame
Mean time to repairMean duration of resolved outages (or warnings), censored starts excludedAt least 3 incidents
Mean time between failuresHours of uptime ÷ number of outagesAt least one outage
Paper refill timeMean duration of paper-out outagesAt least 3 incidents
Tray empty timeMean time a single tray reports empty while others may still printAt least 3 incidents
Burn rateConsumable points used per day, from the last 14 daysAt least 3 days observed
Data quality30% freshness + 40% completeness + 20% validity + 10% station coverageAlways

Below the evidence threshold the dashboard says "insufficient data" rather than showing a number that would swing wildly with one more incident.

4.2 Model inventory

QuestionMethodWhy this method
When will this part reach end of life?Theil–Sen regression on readings since the last replacement, with a 90% intervalRobust to sensor blips; falls back to burn rate with few readings
Is today's fault count normal?Statistical process control c-chart (mean ± 3√mean) and the Western Electric 8-in-a-row rule; Poisson tail test per station-dayCounts of rare events; the standard Six Sigma control tool
How fast are outages fixed, in and out of desk hours?Kaplan–Meier survival curves with a log-rank testKeeps still-open outages as censored rather than dropping them
Do busier printers fail more?Ordinary least squares, outages per week on usage, with residual flags beyond 2σSeparates "busy" from "bad unit"
Which printers fail most, fairly?Empirical-Bayes Gamma–Poisson rates with 90% credible intervals; report-card grades shrink toward the campus rateSmall samples don't top the list by chance
Did several printers fail together?Clusters of outages within 10 minutes across 3+ stations, tested against a Poisson rate for that hour and day typeTells a network or Wepa-side event from a busy moment
Which downtime cost the most printing?Outage hours weighted by that printer's typical use at that time of weekAn hour down at 2 PM on a Tuesday matters more than at 4 AM
Does humid weather go with more jams?Mantel–Haenszel rate ratio, stratified by 6-hour block and weekday or weekendControls for the fact that humidity and printing both follow the clock
What if a desk stayed open later?Monte Carlo replay (1,000 runs) of real outages with extra desk coverage; fix times drawn from real desk-hour fixesAnswers staffing questions with the campus's own history
Where would one more printer help most?Greedy one-step p-median on walking times, weighted by busy-weighted downtimeDirectly minimizes students' extra walking
Supplies to reorderMonte Carlo of recent daily usage: how many of each part to stock at 50%, 90% and 95% confidence; reorder points use the supply lead time (7 days unless set)Gives a range, not a single guess
Which printer may fail in the next 24 hours?Machine learning, belowOnly if it beats the simple alternative

4.3 The outage-risk model

Each printer-hour becomes one example: time of day and week, whether a desk is open and how long until it opens, whether classes are in session, the kind of place, recent faults and warnings, recent usage, part levels. The label is whether an outage started in the next 24 hours.

4.5 The AI analyst

The Assistant is optional and runs only with an API key. It is an analyst with 16 read-only tools over the same data the pages use (station profiles, availability, incidents, repair times, supplies, usage, the report card, period comparisons, campus context, statistics, the risk model, investigations and the app's self-check).

  • Facts only. Every number it writes is checked against the tool results it received, allowing only honest unit restatements (minutes to hours, shares to percentages). Unsupported figures trigger one rewrite; anything still unsupported is flagged.
  • Read-only. It cannot change records, start updates or contact anyone.
  • Safe output. Answers are shown without links, images or HTML.
  • Scoped and fair. BSU printing only; never guesses who caused a problem; says "demo data" when it is.

5.2 How the outage-risk model is scored

Walk-forward
Train on everything before a week, test on that week, for each of the last four weeks. The model never sees the future it is tested on.
Brier score
Mean squared error of the predicted probabilities; lower is better. Skill is the improvement over the baseline's Brier score.
AUC
How well the model ranks printers that did fail above those that didn't; 0.5 is a coin flip.
Calibration
Predicted chance versus observed rate in bins, shown as a chart, so "20%" means about 1 in 5.
Track record
Live predictions are logged and later compared with what happened.
For data scientistsThe baseline is deliberately strong: each printer's recent outage rate, shrunk toward the campus rate. An early version's baseline scored below 0.5 AUC because raw per-printer rates were noisy; shrinkage fixed it. A model that cannot beat this baseline is not shown.
For leadership"Not enough evidence yet" is a correct answer. The risk model stays hidden on new deployments until the data supports it; the page explains which condition is not met.

5.3 Software verification

  • 190 automated test cases run on every push and daily: the page parser against saved real pages, incident rules, the replacement rule, desk hours, the calendar, stories, Ask the data, routing (including an exact Held–Karp check), investigations, accounts and sign-in, security probes, the update pipeline, backup collectors, threat intelligence and durations.
  • Lint (ruff), static security analysis (bandit) and known-vulnerability scanning (pip-audit over the exact lock file) in continuous integration.
  • Attacker-style tests: every route without a session, path traversal, oversized uploads, cross-site posts, password guessing, forged or replayed backup messages, tampering with the audit trail.

5.4 Known limits

  • Usage is inferred from consumable levels; Wepa's job counts are not available.
  • Several alert codes are inferred from Wepa's legend until they appear on BSU's page.
  • Forecasting across semesters (ARIMA and similar) is deliberately postponed until a full academic year of live history exists.
  • Demo data is synthetic. On it, the models mostly rediscover patterns built into the simulator.
  • No multi-factor authentication yet; the administrator is a shared account.

Sign in with the account your ResNet administrator gave you. The left side lists every page; the top bar holds the filter, the time period, export, the activity log, notifications, appearance and the Assistant. Everything is read-only except investigations, your own account and feature requests.

8.1 For students: finding a printer that works

  1. Scan the QR sign on the printer, where your residence hall or building has one.
  2. The page shows whether that printer is working right now and, if not, the nearest printers that are, with the walking time. East Campus Commons counts as open to everyone; other residence-hall printers need card access.

Student status pages are behind sign-in unless the administrator turns on public status pages (with Wepa's and BSU's agreement). They show the present only, never history.

  1. Open the app's address and sign in. A temporary password from the administrator must be changed the first time.
  2. Use a password of 12 or more characters; a few unrelated words works well. Common and breached passwords are refused.
  3. Under your picture: My account (picture, phone, password), Directory, Suggest a feature, Sign out.
  4. Choose an appearance (BSU, light, dark or automatic; larger text; compact; reduced motion; extra contrast). On a BSU football game day the BSU appearance becomes Go Bears, with the game in a banner. Persistent tappers may find a surprise or two.

8.4 Common tasks

Respond to an outage

  1. On Overview, open the top item in the work queue, or go to Rounds for a route.
  2. Fix it on site. The station turns Operational within a minute of Wepa's page updating.
  3. The outage resolves itself with its reference number, duration and desk-hour split. Nothing to type.

Work an investigation

  1. Open Investigations. Detector-opened cases are marked; you can also open one yourself.
  2. Assign it (moves to Analyze), record a root cause (Respond), record the action taken (Review).
  3. A second person closes it. Download the evidence packet (PDF) for a Wepa case.

Report the month

  1. Open Executive, pick the month, read the highlights.
  2. Use Export in the top bar for PDF, Excel, CSV or JSON of the current filter and period.

Suggest a feature or report a problem

  1. Under your picture, choose Suggest a feature.
  2. Pick the kind, give a title and details, and send. It gets a REQ number and becomes a GitHub issue; your name is not included unless the administrator turned that on.

8.6 Frequently asked questions

Why does a station say "No signal"?
It hasn't appeared on Wepa's page for 5 minutes. Its status is unknown, and that time is left out of every rate.
Why "support unavailable until …"?
The team that supports that printer has no desk open right now; the time is when it next opens.
Why does a figure say "insufficient data"?
Too few incidents or too little observed time to be reliable. It appears once there is enough evidence.
Why isn't the outage-risk model shown?
It hasn't beaten the simple baseline on held-out weeks yet; the page says which condition is not met.
Is "In progress" ever shown?
No. The monitor can see that a problem is open, not whether someone is working on it, so it says Ongoing.

Printing, with a warning.

Exploit Prediction Scoring System: probability a CVE is exploited in the next 30 days.

Mean time between failures; mean time to repair.

Time covered by readings, at most 5 minutes per reading.

Cannot print (a red row on Wepa's page).
